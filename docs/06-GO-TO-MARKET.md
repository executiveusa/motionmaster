# GO TO MARKET — MOTION MASTER

## 0. The hard truth this plan is built around

The closest analog to what you asked me to build has **$58 in verified all-time revenue**
(motionvideo.xyz, a $29 skill pack, from a founder with a large audience and a free
open-source site). Two sales.

Meanwhile the category is enormous: **Higgsfield crossed $500M ARR**, ~85% of its usage is
social media marketers, ~80% of that is commercial content for brands. Sora shut down in
April 2026 on $2M lifetime revenue against roughly $1M/day of compute. Runway raised at
$5.3B and pivoted out of commercial video entirely.

Read together, those facts say two things:

1. **Generative video is a scale game, and it is a brutal one.** Do not compete there.
2. **The "pack of AI skills for developers" form does not find buyers.** The motion-design
   audience is small, well served by free templates, and reluctant to pay for prompt advice
   when the same advice is on YouTube and in free repos.

So the motion is not "sell skills harder." The motion is **change who buys, what they buy,
and what they get when it arrives**.

---

## 1. Who we are actually selling to

**Primary — the marketing lead at a small company who has a launch date and no designer.**

They are not a designer. They have tried Canva (everything looks like a template) and
CapCut (they cannot make it look designed). They have tried an AI video generator (it
looks like AI). They are on a deadline. They will pay $150–$500 to avoid a week of
back-and-forth with a freelancer.

**Secondary — the technical creator** who installs the plugin for free, ships a clip,
and either upgrades or evangelises it. This is our reference audience and our review
panel, not our revenue.

**Not our customer, and the page must not pretend otherwise:** the professional motion
designer. They already own After Effects, they have taste, and they will not be moved by
a token system. Leaving them alone is what lets us speak plainly to the primary.

## 3. The distribution we can actually execute

### 3.1 The plugin is the top of funnel — and it is free

One HTTP MCP server and one plugin package install into **ChatGPT, Codex, Claude,
Claude Code, Cursor, Grok, VS Code, Amp, Droid, Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Kimi Code, Kiro,
ZCode, MiMo Code, DeepSeek Harness, Pi and Devin.** The format is proven — the market
leader already ships this exact distribution (MIT, readable).

This is a **$0 CAC channel that reaches the exact people who can evaluate the product** and
tell us what is wrong with it. It is not a marketing spend; it is a packaging decision.

**The install is the demo.** Paste one prompt into ChatGPT, get a video. No landing page
visit, no signup, no trial. That is the fastest possible path from "curious" to "I made a
thing with this."

### 3.2 The gallery is the search engine

Every clip ships with the exact prompt that made it, the stack, and the duration — and a
one-click "Start from this prompt."

That turns the landing page into a library of *working starting points*. It is the same
mechanic motionvideo.xyz proved gets traffic, except ours produces instead of only
browses, and every entry carries a copyable prompt.

**SEO surface this creates, honestly stated:** the long tail of
`remotion launch film prompt`, `gsap kinetic type reveal`, `how to make a product demo
video with code`, `remotion vs after effects for simple motion graphics`. Low volume, very
high intent, no competition.

## 4. The launch

**Pre-launch (2 weeks)** — the gallery is live and free. 8–10 clips, each with its
prompt. No paywall, no email gate on the gallery. It has to be genuinely useful on its
own, because that is what the SEO and the sharing both depend on.

**Launch** — Product Hunt + Show HN + a design-community post, all pointing at the
gallery rather than the checkout. One real offer: the launch price, and a **genuine**
quantity cap (the first 100 licences), because a real cap is honest and a fake countdown
is a lie we would have to defend in a review thread.

**Post-launch (90 days)** — the compound engine:
1. Every clip published with its prompt → search + social.
2. Every prompt run by a stranger → a new clip from a real user → the gallery grows
   without us making it.
3. Every install of the plugin → a user who can upgrade at any time.

### Numbers worth watching

| Metric | Why it is the right one |
|---|---|
| Gallery → checkout click rate | The only honest test of the offer |
| Plugin installs | Reach, and the top of the funnel |
| Clips published by non-founders | The growth loop actually working |
| Refund rate | The one-time price's real failure signal |
| Support requests per buyer | Whether $99 is enough to cover the cost of helping them |

**Do not track vanity.** No "user count" claim until there is one.

## 5. Unit economics

**Price:** Solo **$99 one-time**. Studio **$399 one-time**.

The founder set $99 after the validation card: early buyers want a lifetime download, and
$99 is a price a non-technical buyer can justify against one freelance motion designer
(typically several hundred dollars) without a spreadsheet. It is also under one season of
Runway, which makes "one payment, yours forever" a ten-second argument rather than a
paragraph. $99 remains a hypothesis to test against $149 and $249, not a finding.

**Cost per buyer:** a hosted render costs a few cents of CPU; a $99 buyer who renders 20
videos costs us well under $1 in compute, and a local-render buyer costs us **$0** because
their machine does the work. Support is the real cost, and it is why the edit surface
matters as much commercially as it does creatively: a product a user can adjust without
asking us is a product with near-zero support cost.

**Gross margin:** high, and the reason a one-time price is viable at all.

**The honest comparison:**

| | Motion Master | A $15/mo AI video tool |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 revenue | $99 | $180 |
| Year 2 revenue | $0 | $180+ |
| Year 3 revenue | $0 | $180+ |
| Credits | None | Burn fast, 12 credits/second |
| Output ownership | Yours, including the source | Yours, the video only |
| If you stop paying | Nothing breaks | Resolution drops, watermark returns |
| Vendor risk | Remotion/GSAP are open source | The model can be retired |

**This table is the objection answer, and it belongs on the page.** We do not get to claim
"it is cheaper over time" — $99 is about six and a half months of Runway. We get to claim **"you own it
and it never breaks"**, which is a different and stronger claim, and which is only true
because the motion is code.

## 6. The risks, stated plainly

| Risk | How bad | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The non-technical buyer cannot evaluate motion well enough to direct it | **Load-bearing.** If this fails, the product has no floor. | Ship the edit surface. Test with 5 non-technical users: one clip, one sentence of feedback, count the rounds to "good". |
| One-time price in a monthly category | High | Answer it in the first screen with the ownership table. The offer is the licence + the file, not "cheap per month". |
| No social proof, ever, for a while | Medium | Do not claim proof we do not have. Let the product be the proof. |
| AGPL boundary | Medium, legal not product | Bridge isolated, AGPL, clean-room. Dual-licence if the founder owns the repo. |
| The one-time price means no recurring revenue | Medium | Add a hosted render allowance later — priced honestly, never auto-charged. |
| The technical crowd does not want to pay for a skill pack | **Proven.** motionvideo.xyz proved it. | Do not sell to them. Sell to the person with the deadline. |

## 7. What we do **not** do

- No "trusted by" logo row, no testimonials, no user counts, until they are real.
- No urgency we did not create. A real cap is fine; a fake countdown is not.
- No subscription for the software. The one-time price is the whole reason this product
  is different from every AI video tool, and it is the reason a buyer will take the risk.
- No AI-generated stock footage standing in for product proof.
- No comparison claims about named competitors. We say what we do.
- No attempt to win the professional motion designer. They do not need us, and trying
  costs us the clarity that makes the page work.
