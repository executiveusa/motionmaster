# PROVEN-BETTER-NEW CARD — Motion Master

Mode: **Blunt** (founder-selected). Frameworks: Mark Pincus, *Life at the Speed of Play*.
Pincus's numbers (95%/25%, 9–10 of 10, ~20%) are his heuristics, not measured statistics.

```
PROVEN-BETTER-NEW CARD — Motion Master
Instinct   : People who need professional-looking video for their business want to make it
             themselves, without learning a tool that takes years to master.   [durable — keep]
Primary    : Motion Array / Envato Elements motion-graphics template libraries —
             audience match: their buyers are SMB marketers, indie founders, and designers
             who need motion graphics now and refuse to learn After Effects. That is
             literally our paid buyer, and they browse-preview-buy-ship every week.

Proven — think about (min 4, spanning the dimension sweep):
  - Zero-install first run, owned by Canva/CapCut (acquisition): the buyer must reach a
    finished video from the app they already open every day. Motion Master runs inside
    ChatGPT by pasting a URL — no account, no terminal, no install. How long is our
    time-to-first-video for a buyer who has never written code? If it exceeds 10 minutes
    we lose them to Canva.
  - The brief → generate → tweak → export core loop, owned by Higgsfield (core loop):
    $500M ARR, 85% of usage is social media marketers, 80% of that is commercial content.
    Our loop must be brief → storyboard → preview → export, with a credit meter visible
    to the buyer, or we are guessing at their mental model.
  - The "one brief, many ratios" repurposing mechanic, owned by OpusClip (core loop,
    secondary analog): 1 credit per minute of source, $15/mo, 60 free credits/mo.
    Higgsfield's data says the money is in social marketers, and their pain is one idea
    shipped as 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9. Does Motion Master own the DESIGN half of that loop
    (we do not need to own the cutting half)?
  - The board/timeline as the return surface, owned by Higgsfield + Runway (retention):
    people come back to re-cut, not to generate. Montage's Backlot board already is a
    board. If Motion Master has no persistent project board, we have no retention mechanic
    and a one-time price becomes a one-time relationship.
  - Credit balance with rollover and visible burn rate, owned by Runway (monetization,
    secondary analog): Standard $15/mo, Max $76/mo, 1-month rollover at Max only.
    Our hosted-render allowance needs the same legible economics, or buyers will fear
    the meter and refuse to buy.
  - The one-HTTP-MCP-plus-one-plugin-folder distribution, owned by motionvideo-mcp
    (distribution): one URL serves ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Grok, VS Code, Amp, Droid,
    Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Kimi, Kiro, ZCode, MiMo, DeepSeek Harness, Pi and Devin.
    This ships today and is free reach. We should copy this distribution exactly rather
    than invent a channel.
  - The free public gallery of real work WITH the prompt behind it, owned by
    motionvideo.xyz (content moat / top of funnel). Their /creatives gallery is a
    real mechanic: people arrive to browse, then want the prompt. But note what they
    did NOT build: their catalog MCP is read-only. It browses. It never makes anything.
  - Delivery by a versioned plugin + public repo so updates arrive automatically,
    owned by Expo/dotnet/contentstack agent-skill repos (delivery). Motion Video
    delivers by "accept the GitHub invite and copy the skills folder" — a manual step a
    non-technical buyer cannot perform. If we copy their delivery we inherit their
    ceiling, and we will fail for the wrong reasons.
  - The mechanic the pitch never mentioned: COMMERCIAL LICENSING. Envato and Motion
    Array sell templates for client work, and buyers ask "can I use this for a client?"
    before they buy. Our offer must state plainly that the output is theirs to use
    commercially, or we lose the agency and freelancer segment, who are exactly the
    people who rebuy.
  - The mechanic the pitch never mentioned: UPDATE PROMISE. A one-time price implies
    lifetime updates. Nobody has stated what "lifetime" means, or what happens to the
    buyer when Remotion has a major version. Undefined, this becomes the first
    cancellation trigger.

Better — think about (min 4):
  - Custom motion without a timeline, and the file is yours — versus Envato, where you
    buy a template and bend it, and the output still looks like a template. Does an
    Envato buyer say an emphatic yes? Probably 7–8, not 9–10, because the buyer's
    real 9–10 is subtler: "I changed the words and the color in 30 seconds and it still
    looked designed." WHICH MEANS our product must have a no-code edit surface for
    copy and color. If our only edit path is a prompt typed into a chat box, we are at 7.
    Test: build the "change the headline, keep the motion" path before anything else.
  - Zero install, inside ChatGPT, and the video comes out — versus Canva's magic
    design and CapCut's templates. Canva's users churn on "everything looks like a
    template." If we are genuinely custom, this clears 9–10 for a Canva-churned user.
    Evidence to gather: 5 Canva users asked to make the same 15s launch clip, count
    how many say our output is theirs rather than a template.
  - The prompt is the product — every clip ships the exact prompt, the stack, and the
    duration, copyable, so a buyer can start from a real starting point instead of a
    blank brief. Motion Video proved this mechanic gets traffic; they did not prove it
    converts. Worth 8–9 for a buyer with a deadline.
  - Custom AND editable as code AND owned forever, versus Higgsfield: they give
    custom video but it is model output, not a system you can restyle, and $15/mo
    forever. The 9–10 version is "I paid once and I can restyle it in year two."
    Evidence: does the buyer understand the difference between a video and a video
    project? Most non-technical buyers do not. The landing page must make it legible.
  - ADJACENT VARIANT the pitch never mentioned, surfaced by research: the
    MOTION-DESIGN-SYSTEM licensing variant — sell the motion system itself to people
    who already own a tool. Motion Array, Envato, and premium After Effects template
    sellers (Premium Video Templates and similar) all sell reusable motion systems to
    designers who will not switch tools. A one-time "Motion Master for After Effects /
    Figma / Canva" tier could reach buyers who will never install an agent. Same
    instinct, different delivery, different buyer.
  - What evidence would settle each: 5 user tests on the no-code edit path; 3 landing
    A/B tests on "template-shaped" vs "custom" framing; a pricing survey at $99/$99/$249.

New — think about (min 4):
  - The buyer is not the operator. The agent makes aesthetic decisions the buyer
    cannot evaluate. LOAD-BEARING. If the output is wrong, a non-technical buyer has
    no way to course-correct except by describing what they dislike — and describing
    "the timing feels off" is the exact skill we are selling. The product's core
    promise rests on an unproven assumption: that a non-technical user can direct
    motion through plain language well enough to get something they want.
    Smallest atomic test: give 5 non-technical people one clip and one sentence of
    feedback; measure how many rounds to a result they call good.
  - The hosted render service for non-technical buyers (unproven; load-bearing if
    they cannot run a local renderer). Smallest test: a hosted render of a 15s clip,
    priced at cost-plus, for 10 strangers, and see if anyone pays twice.
  - The one-time-license-with-allowance hybrid (one-time price + recurring hosted
    renders). Quarantined? No — it is the entire revenue model. If buyers hate recurring
    anything, we have no monetization for the hosted path. Test: two checkout pages,
    one-time-only vs license-plus-allowance, 100 visitors each.
  - The multi-agent "crew" as a paid, visible Orchestrate mode. Currently quarantined
    to zero (invisible infrastructure), which is correct. It only becomes load-bearing
    if we market it.
  - The Montage bridge as a product feature at all. AGPL, no demand signal, and a
    buyer who wants "mix real footage with motion graphics" is a narrower, more
    professional buyer than our primary. Isolated in bridge/, AGPL-licensed, sold as a
    Studio-tier line item — which is the correct quarantine.
  - ChatGPT + Codex plugin publication and its paid handling. OpenAI publishes public
    plugins to a universal directory shared by ChatGPT and Codex, and the MCP server
    is ours to run and to bill. Whether a per-seat or per-render paid tier survives
    platform policy is unverified.
  - The whole "one-time, no subscription" promise in a category where every analog
    bills monthly. Not quarantined — it IS the product. Test it, do not assume it.

Sharpest tensions:
  1. Your one-time price sits against a category that bills monthly. Runway at $15/mo
     means $99 is roughly six and a half months of Runway. The page must answer this in the first
     screen, and the answer has to be true: you own the project and the file forever,
     there are no credits, no model limits, and no watermark. If the answer is only
     "it will be cheaper over time," the one-time price loses to Runway every time.
  2. Your buyer's real want is not "custom motion." It is "I changed the words and it
     still looked designed." If the product's only interface is a chat box, you are
     building the 7–8 version, not the 9–10 version, and the 9–10 is the difference
     between a product people try and a product people buy twice.
  3. Your differentiator IS the unproven part. The skills, the token system, and the
     distribution are proven and copyable — the competitor has them. What is unproven is
     that a non-technical buyer can direct an agent through a medium they cannot see.
     If that fails, the product has no floor, because the copy you can prove
     ("it writes motion in code") is the part a technical buyer can already do for free.
```

## What the founder decides next

Three decisions this card forces, none of which I can make:

1. **Do we ship the no-code edit surface in v1?** (copy + color + duration, no code). I say yes — it is the difference between 7 and 9, and it is what the buyer actually asked for in the template-library reviews.
2. **Do we keep "one-time" or offer a monthly floor?** I say keep one-time for the license, and be explicit that the hosted render allowance is a separate, smaller, recurring line — never hide it.
3. **Do we kill the Montage bridge from v1 and ship it in Studio tier later?** I say keep it in the architecture, ship the claim late, and never let it appear in the hero.
